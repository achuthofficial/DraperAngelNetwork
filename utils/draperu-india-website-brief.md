## 2. The Headline Fact

> **27,000 sq ft — the largest Draper Startup House in the world.**

This is the single strongest hook on the site and the one that justifies a 3D treatment. The space is not a coworking floor; it is a multi-level campus that people live in, work in, and run events in, around the clock. If the 3D build has one job, it is to make 27,000 sq ft *feel* like 27,000 sq ft.

## 3. Origin Story (for an About / timeline section)

Two lineages converge in Hyderabad:

**The academy line — Draper University**
- Founded **2012** by venture capitalist **Tim Draper**.
- Private, for-profit institution based in **San Mateo, California**.
- Known for unconventional, real-world entrepreneurship education.
- Signature programmes: **Hero Training**, **Fundamentals of Entrepreneurship**, **GO! Entrepreneurship Immersion Program**.
- Method includes simulations, survival training, hackathons, team challenges, and headline speakers — designed to push founders out of their comfort zones.

**The hospitality line — Tribe Theory → Draper Startup House**
- Began as **Tribe Theory**, a hostel network built on the idea of *hospitality as the foundation of an entrepreneurial ecosystem*.
- Tim Draper made a strategic investment; the network **rebranded to Draper Startup House** and became the international division.
- Founder **Karan Bahadur** — a serial entrepreneur and community builder whose background runs through psychology, hospitality (Holiday Inn, Le Meridien Bangalore), PR at The Times of India, and a decade in Dubai luxury retail and real estate before returning to India in 2008.
- **January 2021:** Draper's team acquired the business outright.

**Where it stands now**
- Operational in **17 countries**.
- Major hubs: New York, Seoul, Latin America, Bangalore, **Hyderabad**.
- Plans announced for **7–8 more spaces across India**.
- **600–700 founders** walk through DSH doors globally every month.
- Rebranded locally to **DraperU India** in the 2025–26 window.

**The mission line (use verbatim — it's the brand's own):**
> "Enable 1 million entrepreneurs by 2030."

Karan Bahadur's framing, also usable as a pull quote:
> "DSH is a movement, not just a space."

## 4. What They Actually Do

### 4.1 Draper Founder Program (DFP)
The **flagship founder development programme** of DraperU India. Supports ambitious entrepreneurs through:
- Mentorship
- Community access
- Startup growth opportunities
- Funding pathways

Places in DFP are awarded as prizes at pitch competitions — so it functions as both a programme and a trophy. Good candidate for a dedicated site section with an application CTA.

### 4.2 Residential Cohort Programme
- **10-day live-in residential programme.**
- International experts teaching a live-in founder cohort.
- Culminates in a **pitch event**.
- Has run with **scholarships attached** (a reported ₹12.5 lakh scholarship pool in a 2025 edition — verify current figures).
- Live-in format is the differentiator: founders eat, sleep, and build in the same building.

### 4.3 Coworking
Positioned explicitly as more than desks. Official framing:
> Tailored workspaces and flexible memberships that adapt to your journey — from early-stage founders to scaling teams.

- Dedicated desks listed **from ~₹7,999/month** (via myHQ — third-party listing, verify direct pricing).
- Ground-floor coworking area is singled out in reviews as calm and comfortable to work in.
- 24/7 access.

### 4.4 Stays / Residence
- **87 rooms**, **11 room types** — dorms through private rooms.
- Rates listed from **~$13/night** on OTA channels.
- Check-in 2:00 PM · Check-out 11:00 AM (early/late subject to availability).
- Guests must be **18+**; under-18s only with a parent or guardian.
- Photo ID required at check-in. Property does not host bachelor/bachelorette parties.
- Mixed audience: founders, digital nomads, remote workers, solo travellers, and people on work stints.

## 5. Brand Voice

Pulled from their own copy. The tone is warm, informal, slightly irreverent, and community-first — **not** corporate-accelerator.

Actual phrasing from the official Hyderabad page:
- *"We invite you to our humble abode tucked in Gachibowli."*
- *"Come live and co-work with us, get sh\*t done 24/7 (if you need)."*
- *"Our house is an all-encompassing home for entrepreneurs and the startup ecosystem."*
- Audience called out by name: *"Founders, startuppers, investors, creatives, innovators, and operators."*
- Verbs they use: exchange, brainstorm, be inspired, be empowered, collaborate, connect.

**Implication for the 3D build:** the word they keep returning to is **house**, not *facility*, *centre*, or *campus*. A warm, lived-in 3D environment beats a cold architectural render. Think inhabited, not showroom.

## 8. Social Proof

**Recurring positives — usable as testimonial themes:**
- Affordability relative to quality — "one of the most professional and affordable stays in Hyderabad"
- Community: "great place to meet enthusiastic entrepreneurs"
- Ideal for solo travellers and long stays ("a night or even for a month")
- Clean, spacious dorms with real storage
- Supportive, responsive staff; late check-in possible
- "Well-organised facilities... conducive to learning, networking, and personal growth"
- Calm and quiet despite a young crowd
- Separate washrooms preferred over attached
- Well-decorated, rooftop coffee shop, good Wi-Fi, clean common spaces

**Recurring negatives — do not paper over these in copy; they set expectations:**
- Mosquitoes in the common area (reported as significant enough to prevent working there)
- AC and fan failures reported in 2026 reviews
- Inconsistency versus earlier years — several long-term repeat guests report decline
- Occasional common-washroom cleanliness lapses
- Isolated reports of poor staff conduct and a cancelled booking
- Staff not always knowledgeable about the surrounding area
- 2 PM check-in seen as late

> **Advice:** don't build a testimonial wall of only five-star pulls. This audience is founders — they read reviews. Honest positioning ("we're a working house, not a hotel") reads better than polish.

## 10. 3D Direction Notes

Grounded in what the space actually is:

- **Journey, not gallery.** The strongest narrative is arrival → work → live → gather. Move the camera through the house in that order rather than offering a menu of disconnected renders.
- **The terrace is the money shot.** It's where the events happen, it has the coffee shop, and it gives you sky, skyline, and a natural end to a scroll-driven camera path.
- **Populate it.** A 27,000 sq ft space empty of people contradicts the entire brand. Even abstract or stylised figures beat a vacant render.
- **24/7 as a lighting device.** Day-to-night transitions across the scroll are on-brand ("get sh\*t done 24/7") and give the 3D scene a reason to change state.
- **Warm materials.** Reviews describe soothing ambience, plants, decoration. Avoid cold grey-glass architectural-viz defaults.
- **Performance floor.** The primary audience is on Indian mobile networks in a tech city. Budget aggressively: draco-compressed geometry, KTX2 textures, a lightweight poster-frame fallback, and a full non-3D content path for reduced-motion and low-end devices. Every fact in this document must be reachable without the 3D scene loading.
- **Two audiences, one door.** Founders applying to programmes and travellers booking a bed want different things. The 3D layer should not obstruct either CTA — keep "Apply" and "Book a bed" persistent in the chrome.
